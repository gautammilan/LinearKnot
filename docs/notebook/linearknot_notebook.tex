\section{Algorithm (current default path)}
Source: the code at \texttt{f90c40a} (\texttt{cpp/pass1/}, \texttt{cpp/pass2/g\_states.hpp}, \texttt{cpp/pass2/sweep.hpp}, \texttt{cpp/pass3/}), \texttt{docs/ARCHITECTURE.md}, \texttt{LINEAR\_PASS2\_SWEEP.md}, and the equations of \texttt{docs/LinearNupack\_math.tex} in the main checkout (cited below as M with its labels).

\paragraph{Symbols.} The sequence is $x_0\dots x_{n-1}$ (0-based, $n$ its length). A pair $i\cdot j$ has $i<j$. $Z$ is the DP03 partition function with pseudoknots (Dirks and Pierce 2003, NUPACK rna1999 parameters, dangles ``some''), $\log Z$ its natural log, and every weight is a Boltzmann factor at $RT=0.6163207755$\,kcal/mol. $\mathrm{at}(i,j)$ is the weight a pair gets as the end of a helix in the exterior loop (terminal AU/GU penalty and dangles), defined when both ends may be terminal (\texttt{end\_ok}). $L=30$ is the loop cap (largest interior-loop gap per side). The beams are $b_1$ (pass~1), $b_2$ (two-index columns and $Q^z$), $b_V$ and $b_G$ (pass-2 maps) and $b_4$ (four-index scan states); a width of 0 keeps every state.

\paragraph{Shape.} Three passes run in order. Passes 1 and 2 build tables that only rank states; pass 3 is the only pass that adds to $Z$. With every beam infinite the scan is exact DP03 (the ranking then decides nothing).

\subsection*{Pass 1: nested pre-pass (beam $b_1$)}
The pseudoknot-free DP03 grammar with the same energy model runs inside over right ends $j=0,\dots,n-1$. Its tables are $Q^b(i,j)$ ($i\cdot j$ paired), $Q^m(i,j)$ (a multiloop interior with at least one branch), $Q^{m2}(i,j)$ (at least two branches; a closing pair reads this one) and $C(j)$, the exterior inside on $[0,j]$. At each $j$ we keep, separately for $Q^b$, $Q^m$ and $Q^{m2}$, the $b_1$ starts $i$ with the largest $C(i-1)\,Q(i,j)$ (the prefix key); a dropped start is never read again. From the kept tables: $C_{\mathrm{pre}}=C$; the suffix weight $\bar S_{\mathrm{pre}}(y)=\bar S_{\mathrm{pre}}(y+1)+\sum_{j>y}Q^b(y,j)\,\mathrm{at}(y,j)\,\bar S_{\mathrm{pre}}(j+1)$ with $\bar S_{\mathrm{pre}}(n)=1$ (M eq:prS); the nested outside weight $\beta^b(x,y)$ of every kept pair; and $\bar M=Q^m$. On an exact pass~1, $\bar S_{\mathrm{pre}}$ is the exterior outside; under the beam it is the suffix of the kept inside. Cost: 1.3\,s CPU at $n=486$ (experiment H).

\subsection*{Pass 2: ranking tables (beams $b_2$, $b_V$, $b_G$)}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Gap partition function.} $Q^z_{\mathrm{pre}}(k,j)$, the nested weight of a gap $[k,j]$; at each $j$ we keep the $b_2$ starts $k$ with the largest $C(k-1)\,Q^z(k,j)$.
\item \textbf{The states sweep} (\texttt{sw\_hyper\_push}, \texttt{-{}-g-sweep states}). One right-to-left pass over the kept pass-1 states, columns $j=n-1,\dots,0$. Each kept state $s$, which is a $Q^m(i,j)$, a $Q^{m2}(i,j)$ or a pair $i\cdot j$, carries two sparse maps: $G_s$ indexed by a pair $(p',q')$ and $V_s$ indexed by a left end $b$. For every pass-1 hyperedge that builds a parent $s$ from a child $c$, the child receives $s$'s maps times the edge weight times the inside weight of the other children. The edges, in the names the binary logs, are: E1 and E2, one unpaired base extending $Q^m$ or $Q^{m2}$; E3, an interior loop (both gaps $\le L$) from an outer pair onto an inner pair; E5 to E8, a multiloop branch $d\cdot j$ (the parent $Q^m(i,j)$ or $Q^{m2}(i,j)$ pushes to the branch pair and to the $Q^m(i,d-1)$ on its left; E5 is the branch with nothing on its left); E9, a closing pair into the nested multiloop table (with E3 multi and E4 multi-ext inside it). At a pair $x\cdot y$ with \texttt{end\_ok} the sweep also adds the stop terms
\[
G_{x\cdot y}[x,y]\mathrel{+}=\mathrm{at}(x,y),\qquad V_{x\cdot y}[x]\mathrel{+}=\mathrm{at}(x,y)\,\bar S_{\mathrm{pre}}(y+1).
\]
Because every parent of a state ends at or right of the state's right end, a state's maps are complete when its column is reached. Then, before it pushes, $G_s$ is cut to its $b_G$ entries with the largest $C_{\mathrm{pre}}(p'-1)\,G_s[p',q']\,\bar S_{\mathrm{pre}}(q'+1)$, and $V_s$ to its $b_V$ entries with the largest $C_{\mathrm{pre}}(b-1)\,V_s[b]$. $Q^m$ and $Q^{m2}$ maps are freed after their column; pair maps are kept and become $G(x,y)$ and $V(x,y)$.
\item \textbf{What the maps mean (identity).} $G(x,y)$ splits the nested outside of the pair $x\cdot y$ by the outermost exterior-loop pair $p'\cdot q'$ around it, and $V(x,y)$ splits it by the left end $b$:
\[
\sum_{p',q'}C_{\mathrm{pre}}(p'-1)\,G(x,y)[p',q']\,\bar S_{\mathrm{pre}}(q'+1)=\beta^b(x,y),\qquad
\sum_{b}C_{\mathrm{pre}}(b-1)\,V(x,y)[b]=\beta^b(x,y).
\]
With $b_V$ and $b_G$ uncapped on RNase~P \emph{D.~radiodurans} 1--150 both hold on all 2948 pairs with \texttt{end\_ok} to maximum relative error $7.816\times10^{-13}$ ($V$) and $7.390\times10^{-13}$ ($G$); \texttt{make check} runs this and fails above $10^{-9}$ (experiment H).
\item \textbf{Arms.} The arm weights the scan reads ($K_L$, \texttt{lK}) are sums over the stored $V$ entries times the pseudoknot suffix $\bar S_{\mathrm{pk}}$ (M eq:pra, eq:prK; \texttt{cpp/pass2/arms.hpp}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Pass 3: left-to-right scan (beams $b_2$, $b_4$, P beams)}
For $j=0,\dots,n-1$ the LR-DP03 inside (\texttt{-{}-pk-l2r on}) computes every cell that ends at $j$: the nested columns $Q^b,Q^m,Q^{m2},Q^p,Q^{mp}$ and $Q^z$ (beam $b_2$), the four-index pseudoknot states $Q^g,Q^{gl},Q^{gr},Q^{gls},Q^{grs}$ (beam $b_4$, one beam per step over all gaps) and the partial states $P_1,P_2,P_3$ (beams $b_2,b_4,b_4$). Each four-index state $X$ with inside weight $\alpha_X$ is ranked by $\sigma(X)=\log\alpha_X+\log\hat O(X)$, where $\hat O$ estimates its outside (M sec:prscore, eq:prO). The growth part of $\hat O$ for a state whose stem has outer pair $x\cdot y$ reads $G(x,y)$ (\texttt{sw\_as\_rn}): every entry is multiplied by the scan's own exterior prefix $\hat C(p'-1)$, the entries are summed over $p'$ for each $q'$, and the $b_G$ right ends $q'$ with the largest $\hat G(q')\,u_L(q')$ are kept, where $u_L$ is the arm weight of the stop term. Keys on the right side read $V$ (\texttt{-{}-grow-r-from-v on}). The $b_4$ states with the largest $\sigma$ survive each step. At the end $\log Z=\log C(n-1)$; pair probabilities come from the scan's outside pass.

\paragraph{Where the time goes.} At the paper preset on Ther ($n=349$): pass~1 0.6\,s, pass~2 213\,s (the states sweep 210\,s, of which the G merge and cut 165\,s), pass~3 167\,s (state scoring 104\,s, table building 57\,s, growth reads 4\,s). Experiment H gives other $b_G$; experiment I compares with the lazy G and explains the difference.

\paragraph{I.3 Why lazy is faster.} Four reasons, in the order of how much work they remove.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \emph{One index instead of two.} Lazy builds a node during the scan, when the scan's prefix $\hat C(p'-1)$ to the left of the node is already final. It multiplies that prefix in at once and sums over $p'$, so a node stores one weight per right end $q'$. Linear runs before the scan, when $\hat C$ is not known yet, so every map must keep each $(p',q')$ entry separately; the read in the scan (\texttt{sw\_as\_rn}) multiplies in $\hat C(p'-1)$ and sums over $p'$ only then. On Ther a read takes 242.7 entries in and gives 46.1 values of $q'$ out, 5.3 entries per $q'$. The same information therefore costs about 5 times as many entries in linear.
  \item \emph{More maps and more pushes.} To satisfy the outside identity (H.1), linear pushes G along every hyperedge of the nested pre-pass grammar, so the $Q^m$ and $Q^{m2}$ states carry maps too: 110{,}950 states and 9.69\,M pushes. Lazy only builds nodes at pairs a root actually reaches (21{,}492 nodes, 38{,}521 roots).
  \item \emph{Lazy cuts the multiloop choices; linear cannot.} Lazy keeps the 16 best multiloop half-steps per node (\texttt{-{}-grow-ml-c 16}) and drops the other 8.43\,M on Ther. Linear keeps every multiloop edge, which is what makes its G exact before the cut.
  \item \emph{Smaller cap.} Lazy keeps at most 32 entries per node. Linear needs $b_G=256$: at 128 it drops the Ther pseudoknot seeds and loses 12.76 (H.2), because its cut ranks seeds by an exterior-loop score.
\end{enumerate}
Together: $1.05\times10^{7}$ lazy terms against about $2.6\times10^{9}$ linear entry merges, about 250 times fewer, and 15 times less growth time (10.8\,s against 164.8\,s). The time ratio is smaller than the work ratio because each lazy term costs more (\texttt{pr\_steps} walks the stem steps per node) while a linear merge is one hash lookup and one \texttt{log\_add} (22\,$\mu$s per push of 271.5 entries).

What linear buys for this cost: G is exact before the cut and checked against $\beta^b$ (H.1); it does not depend on the scan, so it is built once; and its cut is a single beam $b_G$ instead of the lazy truncations (\texttt{-{}-grow-ml-c}, \texttt{trunc\_K}).

\paragraph{L.3 What each step did.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Maps} (\texttt{e6a8e2e}): \texttt{Tab4::Grp::idx}, \texttt{Tab4::vidx}, \texttt{SMap::idx} and \texttt{lArm} are index maps looked up once per state (or per seed); they are now \texttt{ankerl::unordered\_dense} 4.5.0 (vendored, MIT, \texttt{cpp/third\_party/}) behind the \texttt{FastMap} alias, i.e. open addressing over flat arrays with the hash next to the key instead of chaining. Nothing iterates these four maps into a sum, so nothing can reorder. On RP150 this is where the win is: alone it measured 24.77 and 25.67 (lazy) and 35.44 and 37.78 (states) against 27.46 and 38.12, and the merged binary (24.00, 34.95) is not far below maps alone. On Ther the maps-only binary measured 171.97 and 169.88, i.e. base level, not the merged 152.05, so the Ther attribution is open (see the open item below).
  \item \textbf{Reads} (\texttt{e80753f}): \texttt{mu\_at} asked \texttt{v\_map\_kind} for the kind, which looked up the same pair and the same entry, and then looked both up again: two pair lookups and two entry lookups per call on a hit, 65 million times per RP150 run. It now does one of each, with the same \texttt{vm\_i}/\texttt{vm\_ii}/\texttt{vm\_iii} increments on the same calls. Alone: 27.50, 38.12, 169.30 against 27.46, 38.12, 168.95 -- \emph{neutral}. The maps involved are small and hot (4045 pairs, 170{,}414 entries at $n=150$), so the two saved lookups do not show; whether GCC had already commoned the repeated inlined lookups was not checked in the assembly. Kept as an exact simplification, not as a speedup.
  \item \textbf{Log-space skip} (\texttt{67e46db}): \texttt{log\_add} returns \texttt{a} unchanged when \texttt{b - a < (e\_a - 55) ln 2}, because then \texttt{exp(b-a)} is below \texttt{ulp(a)/8} and \texttt{log1p} keeps the added term below \texttt{ulp(a)/4}, inside the half-spacing at which the sum would round away from \texttt{a}; exact, guards for zero, subnormal, inf, NaN and \texttt{NEG\_INF}. Measured neutral (29.35, 42.69, 185.90 against 29.59, 42.55, 185.20), so it is kept as an exact simplification, not as a speedup.
  \item \textbf{Timers} (\texttt{3827ccd}): sub-stage clocks behind \texttt{-DLK\_FINE\_TIMERS} (compiled out by default, so the zeros in the report fields are honest), a new \texttt{[stage] outside} line, and \texttt{PrPartnerTimer} now has a destructor (its field was always 0). Neutral to slightly positive.
  \item \textbf{Batched \texttt{lse} behind \texttt{-{}-lse-batch}: dropped.} The step that removes exponentials from \texttt{log\_add} is neutral on all three cases, so the \texttt{exp}/\texttt{log} pairs batching would remove are not a measurable share; and batching changes the summation order, so it would trade the bit-identity test for a $10^{-9}$ tolerance. The merged binary is bit-identical to the base binary on all four cases.
  \item Open: the \texttt{[stage]} lines account for 21.9\,s of the 29.6\,s user at RP150 lazy, so about 7.7\,s is not attributed to any stage. That is the first thing to nail down before another round of fixes.
  \item Open: the Ther per-step attribution. Each step binary was run on Ther once, hours apart, on a login node that carried other 99\,\% CPU jobs; maps-only (171.97, 169.88) and reads-only (169.30) sit at base level, while the interleaved base-versus-merged pair is 168.95 against 152.05. Interleaving the step binaries on Ther is the missing measurement.
\end{itemize}
